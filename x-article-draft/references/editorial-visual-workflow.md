# Editorial visual workflow for X Articles

Use this workflow only when the article needs a cover or explanatory visuals.

## 1. Lock the editorial system

Write one line for each:

- reader;
- reader outcome;
- central thesis;
- primary evidence;
- desired next action.

Use a new mental model, useful distinction, or practical consequence as the hook. Do not elevate a metric merely because it is large.

## 2. Separate asset roles

- Share post: curiosity and the article link.
- Article title: the question or answer the article will deliver.
- Cover: one recognizable subject and one strong conclusion.
- Inline visual: one relationship that is hard to follow in prose.
- CTA asset: conversion message for the confirmed author; it is not an explanatory figure.

## 3. Extract the subject's visual identity

Record official source URLs for the company, product, person, or place. Prefer official brand guides, sites, repositories, and press assets.

- Use an official logo, icon, interface, or product motif only when usage is appropriate.
- Never generate a fake logo or a near-copy that could be mistaken for an official mark.
- If no usable official asset exists, use a neutral functional symbol.
- Limit the palette to a base color, one official brand color, and at most one semantic color.

## 4. Cover rules

- Canvas: exact 5:2 ratio; default 2000 x 800 pixels.
- Safe area: at least 80 pixels from every edge at 2000 x 800.
- Text: normally two or three groups at most.
- Reading test: identify the subject in one second and the article's point in three seconds.
- Mobile test: headline remains readable when the image is reduced to feed-card size.

Choose one approach:

1. **Tech-news hero** for product launches, developer tools, integrations, and implementation articles. Use `tech-news-hero-cover.md`.
2. **Editorial watermark** for strategy, research, people, and opinion: oversized thesis text in the foreground and a restrained official motif in the background.
3. **Approved reference direction** when the user provides a previously approved example. Reuse the composition principles, not unrelated logos, mascots, or colors.

Avoid generic AI cubes, random circuit boards, decorative dashboards, tiny logos, dense subheadings, and unverified metrics.

## 5. Inline visual rules

Create a visual only when it materially improves understanding.

- Default ratio: 16:9.
- One relationship per image.
- Visual area: roughly 60–75%; text: roughly 25–40%.
- Use one headline, three to five short labels, and at most one conclusion sentence.
- Prefer flows, cycles, layers, selections, comparisons, branches, value shifts, stages, and networks.
- Do not convert paragraphs into decorative cards.
- Do not force ten images. Create up to ten only when the article contains ten distinct relationships worth explaining.

Generate one representative style-anchor image first. Obtain human approval when the visual direction involves non-trivial taste or brand judgment. Then apply the approved background, line style, typography density, and palette to the remaining images.

## 6. Image QA

- Inspect every image at full size and feed-card size.
- Verify Japanese text, product names, numbers, and units.
- Check that arrows and spatial relationships match the article.
- Confirm that official assets are not altered into misleading marks.
- Regenerate or repair misspelled and malformed images before packaging.
- Record the asset source and article insertion point.
